1. NORMAL DRIVE (free roam, no scoring)
---------------------------------------
Terminal 1 — start the server (wait ~30 s):
    bash ~/carla_ws/carla_server.sh

Terminal 2 — open the driving window:
    bash ~/carla_ws/drive.sh
    bash ~/carla_ws/drive.sh --map Town04 --res 1920x1080 --filter vehicle.dodge.charger_2020

MIRRORS (default on): left and right rear-view mirrors are built into the driving window (bottom-left / bottom-right
       insets, mirror-flipped like real glass). MIRRORS=0 bash drive.sh ... hides them. views.sh is still there if you
       want them in a separate window / other views (rear, bev, chase).
WHEEL (default off): MOUSE_STEER=1 bash drive.sh ... draws a click-and-drag steering wheel at the bottom (900 deg lock-to-lock,
       self-centres on release, right mouse button = brake). Tuning: WHEEL_LOCK_DEG=540, WHEEL_RETURN_DEG_S=180.
Keys:  W/S throttle/brake   A/D steer   Q reverse   Space handbrake
       P autopilot   TAB camera view (chase / hood / side / top / low-rear)   ` next sensor (depth/semantic/lidar...)
       C weather   N map layers   Backspace respawn at random spot   H help   ESC quit

Maps:  Town12, Town13  = 10x10 km, highway ring (GTA-style, ~60-90 s to load)
       Town04          = highway loop through mountains (best for speed)
       Town06          = long straight highways
       Town10HD        = dense downtown (default)
       Town01..07, Town11, Town15 also available

Cars:  --filter vehicle.dodge.charger_2020 | vehicle.mercedes.coupe_2020 | vehicle.ford.mustang
       | vehicle.tesla.model3 | vehicle.audi.tt   (Backspace in-window also cycles random cars)

Note: --map loads the map on the server; once loaded, the next drive.sh with the same map is instant.

Second small window with two extra views of your car (works with free roam AND Bench2Drive episodes):
    bash ~/carla_ws/stop_traffic.sh         # stop traffic/chaos only (Ctrl-C in its terminal also works; press twice to force)
    bash ~/carla_ws/views.sh                # left + right side mirrors
    bash ~/carla_ws/views.sh rear bev       # any two of: left right rear front bev chase
ESC in that window or Ctrl+C closes it.


2. DRIVE WITH CARS AND PEOPLE
-----------------------------
Start server + driving window first (steps above), THEN in Terminal 3:
    bash ~/carla_ws/traffic.sh 60 40                   # 60 cars, 40 pedestrians within 250 m of you
    bash ~/carla_ws/traffic.sh 100 60 400 --lights     # 100 cars, 60 people, 400 m radius, headlights on
    (args: N_CARS  N_WALKERS  RADIUS_M  [--lights])

    bash ~/carla_ws/traffic_chaos.sh 60 50 200
        # HAZARD mode: cars are spawned in a RING around you (ahead/behind, your lane + neighbour lanes + opposite lanes), not
        # at the map's spawn points. Inside --chaos-radius (100 m) EVERY same-direction car switches to the aggressive profile
        # (speeds up to 2x limit, tailgates, runs lights); the ones behind/beside you overtake and cut in front of you as soon as
        # their lane is open for 25 m and yours for 20 m; cars ahead brake-check; pedestrians run across. Outside: normal.
        # Prints "[chaos] car N overtaking / cuts in front of you / brakes hard", "pedestrian N runs across" as they happen.
        # Expect contact: NPCs avoid each other but cut-ins are deliberately tight, and a 3 Hz BridgeDrive reacts late.
        # Options: --chaos-radius 100  --max-actors 99  --max-walkers 3     Stop: Ctrl-C or bash ~/carla_ws/stop_traffic.sh
NPCs spawn AROUND YOUR CAR and are topped up every 5 s as you drive away, so traffic follows you.
Ctrl+C in Terminal 3 removes all NPCs.
"Spawn failed because of collision at spawn position" = that spawn point was taken; harmless.
NPC driving is CARLA's built-in Traffic Manager (rule-based, same thing as the P key) — competent, not human-like.

Check who is hero / how many NPCs are near you (while driving):
    ~/miniconda3/envs/b2d/bin/python ~/carla_ws/who_is_hero.py

6. SEE THE SCORE
----------------
    bash ~/carla_ws/show_score.sh ./eval_human      # your drives
    bash ~/carla_ws/show_score.sh ./eval_dev10      # TCP runs
Raw file: ~/carla_ws/Bench2Drive/<save_dir>/result.json

How it's computed:  Driving Score = Route Completion % x Infraction Penalty
Penalty multipliers (per event): pedestrian collision x0.50, vehicle x0.60, static x0.65,
red light x0.70, stop sign x0.80, scenario fail (e.g. not yielding to ambulance) x0.70.
MinSpeed only penalizes being too slow — going fast costs nothing by itself.
"FAILURE" in the header just means at least one criterion failed; the route still scores.

    bash ~/carla_ws/bd_watch.sh 25857                  # run one episode, record chase cam, play it back at 1x afterwards

        # staged "oncoming lane invader": the pedestrian stays, a red Audi on the opposite lane (invader_dist=70 m ahead, invader_speed=6 m/s)
        # drifts invader_offset=1.6 m into our lane once the gap is < invader_trigger=60 m (after invader_start=0.5 s, over invader_in=1.5 s),
        # holds invader_hold=1.5 s, eases back over invader_out=2.5 s. Keep speed + lane = collision; brake or shift right ~1 m = clear.

BridgeDrive OPEN WORLD (random GPS route, no scenarios/scoring) -- needs a running server: bash ~/carla_ws/carla_server.sh
    bash ~/carla_ws/bd_drive.sh --map Town04            # or Town10HD / Town03 / Town05; --dist 600 = min route length

  A viewer window opens (chase cam + top-down), overlays drawn client-side so the net's cameras stay clean:
    GREEN dots + label = the sparse GPS route points the agent receives (one per ~50 m / at every command change)
    RED = current target point the net is conditioned on, ORANGE = next, MAGENTA = destination
    BLUE = the 10 route checkpoints the diffusion bridge predicted this frame (what it steers along)
    thin dark green = dense lane-centre route (what the leaderboard scores against; NOT given to the agent; --no-dense hides)
  CLOCK MODES:
    default: REAL TIME. World/traffic/pedestrians at full speed. The net runs in its own thread (~3 Hz here) and LEAD's own PID
             tracks its latest predicted path at 20 Hz. Perception is ~300 ms stale -> expect collisions in dense traffic and
             rough junctions; that is the latency cost, not a bug. Use lighter traffic: traffic_chaos.sh 30 20 150.
    --sim-rate 3: FIXED SLOW CLOCK. World ticks 50 ms sim steps on a 3 Hz wall timer (never waits for the net). Model sees
             50 ms-spaced frames as trained -> benchmark-like driving, but everything moves at 0.15x (slow motion).
  --sync = lock-step (world waits for the net, ~4x slow motion, every frame processed; what the benchmark does).
  Chaos traffic keeps 3 cars behind you (your lane / next lane) so overtakes + cut-ins happen. QUALITY=Low bash carla_server.sh
  renders faster. ESC or Ctrl-C stops. BD_DEBUG=1 prints the net's target speed/steer/light state each second.

10. BASELINES: CaRL / Roach / Think2Drive  (RL planners from autonomousvision/CaRL, code in ~/carla_ws/CaRL)  [CARL]
=============================
These are PRIVILEGED planners: no cameras/LiDAR. They read a bird's-eye map straight from the simulator (lane geometry,
other cars/walkers, traffic lights, the route) = leaderboard track "MAP". CaRL/Roach = PPO (tiny CNN, ~30 ms/step), so they
run in lock-step at the full 20 Hz in REAL TIME here. Think2Drive = DreamerV3 world model (JAX).
Envs: conda `carl` (py3.10, torch 2.8 cu128), conda `think2drive` (py3.11, jax 0.4.35 + CUDA 12.9 ptxas swapped in for Blackwell).

CaRL / Roach open world (server must be running: bash ~/carla_ws/carla_server.sh):
    bash ~/carla_ws/carl_drive.sh --map Town04                       # CaRL v1.1 (best: DS 73 on longest6 v2)
    bash ~/carla_ws/carl_drive.sh --map Town04 --model Roach_00      # Roach reproduction (DS 22)
    bash ~/carla_ws/carl_drive.sh --map Town10HD --model CaRL_PY_00  # CaRL v1.0
    bash ~/carla_ws/traffic_chaos.sh 30 20 150                       # traffic, in another terminal (world is lock-step: ticked by the agent)
  Viewer: chase + top-down, green = route points, red = current target, magenta = goal. --speed 0 = as fast as possible.
  Note: in the Bench2Drive leaderboard CaRL-class agents get 200 m-spaced route points (vs 50 m for sensor agents).

12. BASELINE: Drive-pi0 / DriveMoE  (CVPR'26 VLA on PaliGemma-3B, Thinklab-SJTU/DriveMoE, code in ~/carla_ws/DriveMoE)  [PI0]
=============================
Drive-pi0 = the embodied-AI VLA pi0 adapted to driving: SigLIP + Gemma-2B backbone (PaliGemma-3B) + a flow-matching action
expert that outputs 10 future trajectory points; inputs = front camera (current + 2 frames back, 224x224) + 5-step history
of speed/accel/yaw-rate + far target point; the text prompt is fixed ("predict trajectory"). Runs the model every 2nd tick
(10 Hz). DriveMoE adds a camera router (picks extra views) and a mixture of skill experts in the action expert.
Leaderboard DS: Drive-pi0-Base bf16 55.9 / fp32 65.9, DriveMoE-Base bf16 74.2 (the authors' 8xH200 numbers).
Env: conda `drivemoe` (py3.10, torch 2.8 cu128, tensorflow-cpu 2.15 (only image augmentation, kept off the GPU),
transformers 4.42.4). Checkpoints: DriveMoE/ckpts/DrivePi0_Base_bf16.pt (11.8 GB), DriveMoE_base_bf16.pt (13.5 GB).
PaliGemma on HF is gated (Google licence) -> only its tokenizer is needed, taken from an ungated mirror
(leo009/paligemma-3b-pt-224, ids verified identical: <image>=257152).
MEMORY (this box: 11.5 GB GPU, 30 GB RAM): the PaliGemma backbone runs NF4-quantised (bitsandbytes, quantize: True in the
local configs) -> Drive-pi0 = 3.7 GB VRAM, ~190 ms per inference; unquantised bf16 is 7.2 GB and does NOT fit beside the
6 GB CARLA the agent's 2x1600x900 cameras need. Expect a few DS points below the paper numbers because of NF4.
Verified here (route 24211, DynamicObjectCrossing): Drive-pi0 DS 50 (hit the pedestrian, RC 100), DriveMoE DS 100 (4.7 GB VRAM,
5 cameras, ~0.46 s per tick in eval vs ~0.22 s for Drive-pi0 -> a 30 s route takes 2-5 min of wall time).
The checkpoint files also hold optimizer states (that is why 12-13 GB); they are memory-mapped at load, otherwise the loader
needs 25 GB of RAM and the kernel OOM-killer takes the desktop down (it did once). The launchers run under a cgroup cap.
